KEEPSAKE · slide onewhat we built
What we built

A two-person arena where the chain remembers one kind of thing.

A 16 × 16 grid, two wallets, five verbs, thirty-centisecond blocks. Moves, shots and health are all on-chain — but the only facts that get written down forever are the ones that cost the player something.

recorded · kind 1
A pact

You stand beside someone and promise not to shoot them. It is witnessed by the arena, not by a server.

recorded · kind 2
A spare

They are at 2 hit points, armed, in reach — and you let them go. This is the one the whole project exists for.

recorded · kind 3
A betrayal

You break the pact. The record carries the ID of the promise it broke, so the two can never be quoted apart.

And an ordinary kill? Nothing. Killing is playing, and scoreboards already exist on-chain — if a plain kill wrote a record, you could farm it by spawning and shooting. The log takes only restraint, and its betrayal. That one refusal is what stops anyone manufacturing credit.

Two contracts · 11,120 bytes of runtime · 20 transactions recorded per match · no token, no treasury, no admin key (the notary's owner is renounced).

KEEPSAKE · slide twowhy we built it
Why we built it

Interesting behaviour has nowhere to live.

the gapA database can say hp = 2. It cannot say why it stayed 2. Every answer to "who was generous under pressure" lives in someone's application tables, and dies when the product does.01
the issuer problemYou can sign a claim about yourself, which makes it worthless. Or you beg an authority to sign for you, which costs a key, a policy and a review queue. Both assume a fact needs an issuer.02
the expiryRounds reset, demos go back to localhost. A record you can only read through the service that produced it isn't a record — it's a customer relationship.03
our answer
No issuer. A witness.

The only thing allowed to mint a fact is the contract that enforces the rules of the moment — so a claim about you cannot be produced by you, and cannot be produced by an admin either.

what that removes
Trust, three ways

You can't certify yourself. We can't edit your record — there is no revoke, no update, no delete function in the log. The operator can't repoint the attester either: that pin was write-once, it was used once, and the log's owner is renounced — no key on earth can retarget it now.

the test we hold ourselves to
Impossible, not discouraged

If it were merely unfashionable to fake a good deed, we'd have built a leaderboard. The rule lives in a NotKillShot revert and an SSTORE — and it refuses the same way whoever asks.

This is not a game about games. It is a working demo of one narrow, useful idea: a public log that only accepts records the author had to risk to create.

KEEPSAKE · slide threeMonad, and the edge
How Monad is used, and what it buys

The pitch dies if the two players wait nine seconds for each other.

Everything here is a plain EVM call on Monad testnet 10143 — Foundry, ethers, MetaMask, the same addresses in the explorer. What the chain changes is not what is possible, it is what is watchable and what is cheap enough to prove.

block time≈303 ms, measured on the public stats — so a Spawn lands in the moment you press it, and the other player's board updates on the next poll. We wait ~2 s, not 9. 303 ms
feesBase fee 102 Gwei. A Spawn costs 57,165 gas metered — six thousandths of a MON. A 21,165-gas transfer cost 0.00215883 MON, which is exactly gas × price: the maths on this slide is arithmetic, not vibes.0.0058 MON
storageA cold SSTORE is 8,100 gas, so the pact and the spare are the expensive turns (≈0.03–0.04 MON) and a walk is free-feeling. Monad also bills the gas limit, so every verb sends an explicit cap.8,100
the game is the oracleWorld calls HonorLog.attest in the same transaction as the move. No relayer, no indexer to trust, no signature collection: the attester is the contract enforcing the rules.1 attester
read pathLog queries cap near 1,000 blocks, so the board rebuilds by scanning Spawned and Attested from the deploy block — and verify(uid) is one eth_call anyone can repeat in the explorer. 1 call
the edge, plainly
Fast enough to be a game

Two humans in a room, no lag, no "wait for the confirmations". If it were slow we'd have demoed it with screenshots, like every attestation product before us.

Cheap enough to prove

"Go verify that yourself" costs a voter nothing — no mint, no gas, no account. Proof has to be free or it's a claim.

Permanent enough to matter

Match-rotation orphans a cell, never a record. The betrayal of a pact outlives the game, the demo, and this weekend.

Honest limits, because a judge will check: we are not claiming throughput — nobody demos 10,000 TPS with two people. What Monad buys here is 300 ms blocks, sub-cent fees, and EVM equality, and that is enough for what we built.

Now the part you can check

Watch me get refused.

Five moves, both wallets live on Monad testnet. The first one is the interesting one.

01

Spawn

Two chairs, two wallets. Each board learns about the other from an event, not from us.

02

Walk & pact

One cell per click. Then a promise — gilt line on both boards within two seconds.

03

Kill, freely

Nothing stands between us. I shoot. Nothing is written. That's the design, not a bug.

04

Pact, then betray

Same shot, one difference. Kind 3, with the ID of the promise it broke.

05

Verify it

Paste that ID into the block explorer, not into my page. It reads the same record.
