Manual 01 · what you are looking atthe page tells you which

The board has four states, and it names its own.

Before anything else: this page never pretends. The strip under the header tells you which of the four you are in, and every one of them is a supported state rather than a broken one.

statehow you get therewhat the page sayscan you act?
offopen it with no wallet and no addresses.json no chain, no recording — paste the World + HonorLog addresses under DEPLOY, then 'Use addresses'no
previewno live board, but the recording ships with the page this is the recorded preview — ⏭ through it, or wire a live World below to move piecesno · replay only

  • The preview is the product's own evidence. It replays a 20-transaction match recorded off solc 0.8.24 against the patched bytecode, from web/demo-match.json. The banner says Replay recorded · not a broadcast so nobody mistakes theatre for a demo.
  • Read-only is the mode 90% of people will use. No wallet, no signature, nothing to approve. If the page cannot reach the RPC it says chain read failed — retrying instead of showing you a confident blank.
  • Nothing here has an admin path. No reset button, no undo, no "as the organiser" — every action is the same World call any player can make.

Manual 03 · step 1 — MONwhat each thing actually costs

One claim funds the whole day. Twice over. Nine times.

Reads are free — watching the board, verifying a UID, the whole read-only experience. Only writes pay, and the fee is small enough that the interesting part is how you pay it.

  1. i.

    Get 50 MON

    At the venue: the in-app claim on the Blitz event page, once per whitelisted email (a second attempt says You have already claimed your 50 MON tokens for this event). Fallback: faucet.monad.xyz. Both drip from one distributor, 50 MON at a time.

  2. ii.

    Fund the second chair by transfer

    There is no second claim to make. Send 2 MON from the deployer to player B — 0.0022 MON to send, 16× what B's whole role costs. Paste the full 42-character address; a 0x1234…abcd display is 8 hex digits, and 8 hex digits is 4 billion collisions across 872k accounts.

  3. iii.

    Then stop thinking about balances

    A zero balance fails on the first broadcast, after a simulation that looked completely fine. A non-zero one never comes up again.

The recorded match is web/demo-match.json: 21 frames, of which 20 are writes (the 21st is a read-only verify(), which is free) and one is the owner's startMatch(), which the page never sends. Caps are the sum of GAS_CAP over exactly those writes.

All converted at the testnet's measured 102 Gwei (base 100 + 2 tip). Cross-checked on-chain: a 21,165-gas transfer cost 0.00215883 MON, which is exactly gas × 102 Gwei.

the one rule that surprises people: Monad bills the LIMIT, not the metered gas
worked example, today
A spawn with --gas-limit 200000 needed 57,165 gas and paid 0.0204 MON — 200,000 × 102 Gwei exactly. Three and a half times the necessary fee for a roomy limit. The page therefore sends explicit caps (the cap sent column, GAS_CAP) and its fee cell counts the limit, not the metered figure, so what it prints is what left your wallet.
so: pad deliberately
Never let a wallet pick a 1,000,000 default. A cold SSTORE is 8,100 gas on this chain, so the caps above are real headroom, not laziness — and a reverted call still costs you the cap.

Manual 05 · step 2 — connectwhat happens when you press the button

Connect does five things, in an order that is not negotiable.

  1. 1

    Read the chain

    eth_chainId first. If it isn't 10143 the page asks your wallet to switch, and only if the wallet has never heard of the chain does it offer wallet_addEthereumChain. Accounts are requested after, so a prompt you see at this point is about the network, not about you.

  2. 2

    Ask for accounts

    eth_requestAccounts. Choose which accounts the site may see — if you intend to play two chairs, select both here or the second tab will be the same address as the first.

  3. 3

    Resolve the board

    In priority order: ?world=…&log=…&block=… → ./addresses.json → localStorage → what you pasted in the DEPLOY panel. Anything under 42 hex characters is rejected with both addresses need to be full 0x…40-hex values.

  4. 4

    Wire both contracts

    From the deploy block forward it scans Spawned and Attested, so the board rebuilds itself from events rather than from any state the page remembers.

  5. 5

    Poll

    ~2 s. There are no pending-state subscriptions on this chain and no eth_syncing; the honest interval beats a websocket that silently dies.

strings you will see, and whether they are problems
wallet ready on Monad testnet · nothing to sign until the World and HonorLog addresses are wired — step 2 of DEPLOY.md
Correct, before a deploy. Not an error: the connect worked, the board doesn't exist yet.
still not on Monad testnet — the board will fail every write until you switch
An error, and a precise one: you may connect and read, but every write would revert on a chain with no game deployed. Switch and try once.
no EIP-1193 wallet in this browser — MetaMask or Rabby
You opened the page in a browser with no extension. Nothing is broken; use a wallet browser to act, any browser to watch.
✗ cannot read that World over <rpc>
The address resolved but the call failed. Usually the endpoint; if the block number in the panel predates the deploy, the scan range is rejected and you'll see RPC refused that range; rescanned the last 900 blocks — a warning, not a failure.
Manual 06 · step 3 — the fieldone click, two meanings

Click a cell. If it is empty, you step there.
If it holds a player, you target them.

your selectionpact line (gilt)spared (moss) killed (wax)orphaned by a rotation

The real board is 16 × 16; this is 8 × 8 so it prints on one page. Marks that outlive the moment are called scars and are drawn from attestation records, not from the current state — which is why they survive a match rotation.

  • Adjacency is Chebyshev-1: any of the eight surrounding cells, diagonals included. Two cells away is not a step — the page says that is not a step — 1 cell in any direction, including diagonal and does not send anything.
  • One cell per click. move(dx, dy) is one step. To cross the field, walk it. The chain reverts Oob for off-board and NotAdjacent for a shot across the room.
  • Spawn once per match. It puts you at a position with full hp and ammo and emits Spawned, which is how everyone else learns you exist.
the concurrency rule, because blocks are 300 ms
one at a time — Shoot has not landed yet
The page will not queue a second write while one is in flight. Fast blocks make getTransactionCount lag behind your own last transaction, so double-clicking is how you get a nonce gap and a stuck-looking board. Await the receipt, then act.
Spawn first — you are not on the board
Every verb except spawn requires you to be a live player in the current match. That is NotInMatch and Dead in the contract, said in English here.
Manual 07 · the verbswhat each requires, and what it writes

Five verbs. Two of them can make a record.

verbwhat it doesrefuses unless…writes to the log?
spawn()enters the current match at a position, full hp, armed you are already live — AlreadyLiveno
move(dx,dy)one cell, any direction it is a real step, you are alive and in-match — Oob · Dead · NotInMatchno
pact(other)you will not shoot them, and they you adjacent, alive, no pact already standing — NotAdjacent · AlreadyPacted yes · kind 1
spare(other)you had the shot and did not take it they are adjacent, at ≤ 2 hp and armed — NotKillShot yes · kind 2
shoot(other)the shot itself adjacent and armed — NoAmmo · SamePlayer only if a pact was standing → kind 3
sealMe()voluntarily records your own conduct, for the record it has not been sealed this match — AlreadySealedyes · a roll-up
why so little is recorded — this is the design, not a missing feature
a kill writes nothing
Killing is playing. If the log recorded kills it would be a scoreboard, and scoreboards already exist on-chain; anyone could farm them by spawning, shooting and re-spawning. What the log takes is the thing that has no incentive: restraint, and its betrayal.
so: what a record costs you
Pact — an option you gave up. Spare — a life you could have taken, at ≤ 2 hp, with ammo, with the whole field watching. Betrayal — the only one of the three that also gains you something, which is exactly why it needs a witness.

Reverts are the point of the demo, not a failure of it: the page shows the contract's own name for your refusal (NotKillShot, AlreadyPacted, NoAmmo…), in wax, with the call it came from. World.sol declares eleven of them.

Manual 08 · the pacta gilt line, and a UID

Propose it when you are beside them. It is not a chat channel.

A pact is an attestation like any other — minted by the World, addressed to both of you, and it stands until one of you breaks it or the match rotates.

  1. i.

    Select their cell

    Gilt outline. If they are not adjacent the Pact button stays disabled rather than letting you discover it by paying for a revert.

  2. ii.

    Press Pact → sign

    pact(other) mints the record and emits Attested. Both boards draw the line within one poll (~2 s) — the other player's page learns from the event, not from yours.

  3. iii.

    Read it back

    livePact(from, to) gives the UID of the standing pact. Load my UIDs lists everything the log holds about you (countOf + uidAt), which is the ordinary way to find it.

the two things people try, and what happens
AlreadyPacted
A second pact between the same pair in the same match reverts. Earlier it would have stacked a second UID and orphaned the first — the guard exists because an orphaned attestation looks like a broken one.
the other player cannot propose it for you
SamePlayer and the adjacency rule mean both parties must be beside each other and one of them must pay. There is no signature to collect and no consent flag to tick: the record is of you doing a thing near them.

A pact you never touch simply stays. There is no un-pact: the only way out is to break it (kind 3, wax) or to let the match rotate, which orphans the cell but never deletes the record.

Manual 09 · the sparethe verb the whole project is about

To be spared, you had to be about to die.

spare(other) is the only action whose value comes from being refused by arithmetic. The button is disabled unless the test passes, and the contract runs the same test again — because the button is a courtesy and the contract is the truth.

the testin the pagein World.sol
adjacentbutton disabledNotAdjacent
they are alive, in this matchbutton disabledDead · NotInMatch
they are at ≤ 2 hpbutton disabledNotKillShot
they still have ammobutton disabledNotKillShot
you are armedbutton disabledNoAmmo

The hp threshold is __spareWindow, two by default, and the hint line quotes it so nobody has to remember it: Spare additionally needs them at 2 hp or less and armed — the chain checks, not this page.

  • Why "they still have ammo"? Because sparing an unarmed neighbour is mercy with no risk. The record is only interesting when the other side could still have shot you back.
  • What it writes. Kind 2, subject = you, other = them, match-scoped. It is the one entry a player can accumulate without hurting anybody, which is the point.
  • What the refusal is for. At the table, hand someone a healthy neighbour and press Spare. The wax line naming NotKillShot is the most convincing thirty seconds of the demo, and it cannot be produced by a video.

Spare does not cost them anything and does not heal them: hp stays where it was. It only records that it could have been zero.

Manual 10 · the betrayalthe one kill that is remembered

Shoot someone you pledged to, and the log chains the two records together.

  1. i.

    A pact stands

    Kind 1, gilt line between you. Without it, shoot is just a turn: hp drops, ammo drops, and the log stays empty.

  2. ii.

    Shoot

    The contract sees the live pact for that pair and, on the same transaction, mints kind 3 with refUID = the pact's UID, and marks the pact broken (pactBroken).

  3. iii.

    Two records, one chain

    The betrayal points at the promise it broke. Verify either UID and you get the other, so "I was witnessed sparing them" and "I was witnessed killing them" can never be quoted apart.

The docket is drawn like the page's own record panel. Broke also increments the kept column's counterpart in the header, and a betrayal of a betrayal takes credit back — which is why "kept" and "broke" cannot both be claimed by the same player for the same pair.

  • The wax scar stays on the board after the match ends, because scars are drawn from attestation records rather than from live state. Replay over: the four UIDs are what remains when the match is deleted — that is the whole product.
Manual 11 · the recordread it here, then read it somewhere else

Any record is eleven fields and one address.

Paste a UID into the Verify field and press verify(). That is HonorLog.verify(bytes32) — a single eth_call, no wallet, no permission. Then paste the same UID into the explorer and compare: this page has no authority over what you read.

fieldwhy it is there
uidthe key. Derived from the content, so it cannot be reused or forked.
schemaone schema for this game; the field exists so another contract can share the log.
attesterthe field that makes it mean something. Must equal the World — verify() returns it so you can require provenance, not just validity.
subjectwhose choice it was
otherwho it was done to
matchIdwhich round. Survives rotations, which is how an orphaned record stays readable.
blockNumberwhen, in blocks — 300 ms each, so it is also a timestamp
refUIDthe pact a betrayal broke
kind1 pact · 2 spare · 3 betrayal
extrathe small print: hp, ammo, coordinates at the moment of the choice
revokedalways false: there is no revoke function.

the difference between the two sentences people confuse
"verified"
the UID exists in a log.
"verified as to provenance"
the UID exists in this log, minted by the pinned World. A record that clears the first and not the second is the attack the write-once setWorld exists to prevent.
Manual 12 · two chairs, or nonea spectator sees everything

It takes two wallets to play and zero to watch.

  1. i.

    Two profiles, not two tabs

    One MetaMask, two accounts, is enough if you select both accounts when the site asks. A second browser profile is more legible at a table: A on the laptop, B on the phone.

  2. ii.

    Both Spawn

    Each from their own chair. The header's on board goes to 2 and the two cells appear on both pages within a poll.

  3. iii.

    Walk into each other

    Adjacency is Chebyshev-1, so it takes as many moves as it takes. This is the part that reads as a game rather than a form.

  4. iv.

    Pact → refuse → spare → shoot

    The four-beat sequence in Manual 09's order. Refuse first; it is the beat the room reacts to.

if you only have one wallet (you will)
the Watch field — opponent address to pin to the board
Paste any address and your page draws what the chain knows about them: position, hp, ammo, their seals, their scars. No signature, no consent, nothing to approve — the information is already public, which is the premise of the whole thing.
the read-only URL
Send a voter https://knarayanareddy.github.io/keepsake/web/ with no wallet at all and they land in Watching the chain: the board, the scars and the numbers, live from the RPC. It cannot change state, so it is safe to hand to a stranger.
  • One player on the field is a real state, not a bug: it is the hint that says "hand the other profile this URL". Nobody on the board yet means exactly that.
  • An address is not a player. Watching someone who never spawned gives you an empty cell and an honest —, not a phantom.
Manual 13 · point it at your contractsno rebuild, no redeploy

The page is not tied to our deployment. Paste yours.

fieldwhat goes in it
World address0x + 40 hex. The one that mints records.
HonorLog addressthe log the World was constructed with.
deploy blockwhere to start scanning events. Ours is 57940359 — 38 blocks before the World's create, deliberately. Leave it 0 to scan from the beginning.

Then Use addresses. It persists to localStorage, which is why a shared machine keeps the board after a reload, and why a query-string link beats it: a URL is reproducible, a stale cache is not.

# the same wiring, for a link you can put in a slide
https://knarayanareddy.github.io/keepsake/web/

  &log=0x24ce27F66C18fF10C5C6B496F64774438cB1F4BC
  &block=57940359
  • Priority is fixed: query string > addresses.json > localStorage > the fields you typed. So a shipped file can be overridden by a link without a rebuild — that is how you demo a second deployment from the same static site.
  • The block number is a scan start, not a filter on truth. If it is after the real deploy block you will silently miss the first Spawned events, and the board will look empty for players who exist. Wide is free; narrow is a lie by omission.
  • ~1,000 blocks per log query is the RPC's ceiling on this chain. If a range is refused the page says so and rescans the last 900 blocks rather than pretending it read everything.
  • Verify the pin yourself: cast call $LOG "world()(address)" must return $WORLD. If it doesn't, that World's attestations go to a different log and the page will show a board with no records — which is the correct display of a broken deployment.

Manual 15 · for the operatortwo levers, one of them one-way

The deployer owns two things. Use both on purpose.

# rotate the match between pitches. Everyone's old cells go
# ghost; every record stays readable, match-scoped.
cast send $WORLD "startMatch()" --legacy \
  --rpc-url monad_testnet

# give up the right to ever call setWorld again. One-way:
# after this, no key can touch the notary. Ever.
cast send $LOG "renounceOwner()" --legacy \
  --rpc-url monad_testnet

Both are cheap: renounce cost 0.0033 MON (32,770 gas). World has no renounceOwner — startMatch stays owner-gated so the board can be reset for the next pair of players. That is the whole reason it exists.

  • Renounce after your last pitch, not before the first. You keep nothing you can get back, and you lose the rotation you may want twice more.
  • The key is the risk, not the contract. Treat it as public the moment it leaves your machine: never in a chat, never in a shell argument (it lands in history and in ps), never committed, never reused across projects. read -rs "PRIVATE_KEY" && export PRIVATE_KEY is the only form this runbook sanctions.
  • A leaked owner key on this system can: rotate the match, and nothing else. It cannot mint, edit, revoke or repoint a record — attest is gated to the World, and the World pointer was write-once. Say that instead of "we are secure".
  • Re-deploying? Deploy.s.sol rewrites web/addresses.json. That file is the only place the site reads addresses from, so keep it single — a second copy somewhere else will go stale and the page will not complain.
